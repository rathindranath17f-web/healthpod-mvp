THE TECHNOLOGY

Molecular sensing, corrected for dilution

Electrochemical measurement gives quantified values, not colour categories. A specific-gravity reading corrects each result for how diluted the bowl sample is. That correction is the core advantage no optical rival has.

The research this stands on

The concept builds on published, peer-reviewed work on passive toilet-based diagnostics, including a Stanford study in Nature Biomedical Engineering that demonstrated the scientific feasibility of the approach. Electrochemical biosensing and specific-gravity dilution correction are established methods. We are combining proven science in a new form, not inventing unproven physics.

Under the hood: what actually happens in the backend

For the technical reader. This is the real signal chain, end to end, from a drop of urine to a number on a phone. Every stage below uses established methods; where a term is unavoidable it is explained in plain words.

STAGE 1 · THE SENSING ELEMENT

Electrochemistry turns a molecule into a current

The cartridge holds a small three-electrode cell (working, counter, reference). For a marker like glucose, an enzyme on the working electrode (glucose oxidase) reacts with it and releases electrons. That produces a tiny electrical current proportional to how much glucose is present. This is amperometry, the same principle as a handheld glucometer. Markers like pH use a potential difference instead (potentiometry). A few markers still use a reagent pad read by a light sensor, the same chemistry as a dipstick, read by the machine rather than the eye.

STAGE 2 · THE ANALOG FRONT END

A clean signal, then a number

The raw current is in the microamp range, so an analog front-end amplifies it, filters out noise, and an analog-to-digital converter turns it into a digital value the microcontroller can use. Temperature is measured at the same time, because reaction rates drift with temperature, and the reading is compensated for it. Nothing here is exotic; these are standard biosensor electronics.

STAGE 3 · CALIBRATION

The cartridge carries its own calibration

Each cartridge batch is factory-calibrated: the relationship between current and concentration (the calibration curve) is measured and stored on the cartridge's authentication chip. When the device reads a cartridge, it loads that curve, so it converts the current to a real concentration accurately, and it knows the cartridge is genuine and which tier it unlocks. This is how one hardware base becomes Lite, Pro or Max by cartridge alone.

STAGE 4 · THE DILUTION CORRECTION (the moat)

Normalise for how watered-down the sample is

A bowl sample is diluted by residual water, so the concentration reads low. The device measures the sample's specific gravity (its density versus water), which tells it the dilution factor. The correction is simple arithmetic: corrected value = raw value × dilution factor. Clinical labs already normalise urine this way (often to creatinine). Doing it in situ, at the moment of reading, is the part rivals do not have.

STAGE 5 · IDENTITY FUSION

Three weak signals combined into one confident decision

The device does not use one sign of identity, it fuses three. Bluetooth signal strength from a registered phone gives proximity. A learned per-user model of weight and time-of-day gives a behavioural match. A rough biochemical baseline (for example the person's usual creatinine and specific-gravity signature) gives a chemical match. Each produces a confidence score; the scores are combined, and only if the total clears a threshold is the reading attributed to that person. Below the threshold it is marked unverified and never scored. This is standard sensor fusion, the same idea a phone uses to decide it is really you.

STAGE 6 · EDGE THEN CLOUD

The device does the fast part, the cloud does the smart part

The on-device microcontroller handles acquisition, calibration, temperature and dilution correction, and a first-pass in-range check, so it works even before it syncs. The corrected, identified reading is then sent encrypted to the cloud, which attaches it to the person's trend, runs the scoring model, checks thresholds, and drives the app and the physician dashboard. Data handling is built to be DPDP-compliant, and only consented data ever leaves the device.

STAGE 7 · THE CLEANING CONTROL LOOP

Actuate, flush, then verify before re-arming

After each use the controller drives a micro-pump and valves to run a water-jet rinse and a channel flush. A verification sensor then reads the cleaned channel against a clean baseline. Only when that check passes does the device re-arm to accept the next person. It is a closed control loop: act, measure, confirm, proceed.

THE WHOLE PIPELINE, IN ONE LINE

Sample to score

Sample → electrode reaction → amplify and digitise → apply cartridge calibration and temperature compensation → correct for dilution → fuse identity → edge first-pass → encrypted sync → cloud trend, threshold and alert → app and physician view. Every arrow in that chain is an established, buildable step.

Honest technical note

The engineering work that remains is making them perform together inside a toilet: repeatability under 10 percent, detection limits below clinical thresholds after dilution, correction accuracy against lab references, and reliable identity fusion in a real household. Architecture, technical documentation and feasibility

How it works, in plain terms

The whole design rests on one choice: measure molecules electrochemically and correct them for dilution, instead of reading colour with a camera. Everything else follows from that. Each layer below is tagged by how proven it is, so nothing reads as a claim it has not earned.

First, the simplest version

Think of the strip a diabetic dips in urine, or the sensor in a glucometer. Health Pod does the same kind of chemistry, but automatically, inside the toilet, once a day, and it fixes one problem those strips cannot: urine in a bowl is watered down, so the raw number looks too low. Health Pod measures how watered-down the sample is and corrects the number back to the true value. That correction is the clever part, and it is why our reading is trustworthy and a colour-strip is not.

How it works, in five everyday steps

STEP 1

A tiny sample is taken

Once a day, a small amount of urine is drawn into a sealed channel, not the open bowl. Like a lab taking a drop, but automatic.

STEP 2

The cartridge reacts

The sample touches sensor pads in a replaceable cartridge. Each pad reacts to one marker, the same idea as a test strip, but read by the machine.

STEP 3

It reads a number

The device turns each reaction into a real number, not a colour. This is the difference between "looks a bit dark" and "5.4".

STEP 4

It corrects for water

It measures how diluted the sample is and adjusts the number up to the true value. This is the step no rival has.

STEP 5

The phone shows it

A simple in-range or out-of-range status and a trend appear in the app. A doctor is alerted only if something crosses a line.

THE CARTRIDGE IDEA

Like ink in a printer

The printer is cheap; the ink is where the business is. Here the toilet is near cost; the monthly cartridge earns. It also unlocks which tests you get.

The same thing, in four technical layers

LAYER 1 · SENSING

Electrochemical array Proven chemistry

An electrode array with enzyme and reagent pads in the cartridge reacts with the sample and produces a quantified value per marker. Enzyme glucose sensing is the most validated biosensor format there is.

LAYER 2 · CORRECTION

Dilution normalisation Prove in-bowl

A specific-gravity reading taken at the same moment corrects for how diluted the bowl sample is. The method is standard lab practice; proving it inside a toilet is the engineering to do.

LAYER 3 · READER

Controller and self-clean Standard parts

Sealed electronics, a low-power microcontroller, Wi-Fi and BLE, and the cleaning system. No exotic parts, which is why India-first manufacture is realistic.

LAYER 4 · INTELLIGENCE

App and cloud AI Established

Turns raw readings into a daily trend score with a physician dashboard. This is what users subscribe to. Raw output alone is not the product; the monitored trend is.

Feasibility check, done honestly

Already proven Exists today

Electrochemical urine sensing is mature and commercial. Specific-gravity dilution correction is standard lab practice. Passive identification by proximity, pattern and biometrics exists in other health devices. None of this is speculative.

Must be proven Our work ahead

Repeatability under 10 percent in the bowl, detection limits below clinical thresholds after dilution, correction accuracy against lab references, zero carry-over after cleaning, and interference from vitamin C and cleaning agents. All ordinary engineering tasks, not fantasies.

How it gets validated

A bench validation partnership with an institution such as IISc Bangalore or IIT Madras, then a hospital pilot through the Institutional tier to confirm real-world performance. That pilot doubles as the clinical evidence buyers say they need.

The one thing we will not claim

No accuracy figure is certified until this validation is done. Everything is labelled by evidence level so the design holds up when it is questioned.

The MVP: a ten-marker urine panel

Health Pod Lite runs a standard ten-parameter urine screen, the same set a clinical dipstick covers, measured by the device and corrected for dilution. Specific gravity is both a marker and the reference used to correct the others.

An early sign of kidney stress, important given India's diabetes and hypertension burden.

Shows fat-burning metabolic state, relevant to diabetes and fasting.

A liver-function indicator.

A second liver-and-bile indicator, read alongside bilirubin.

Honest note on method

Glucose, pH and specific gravity are the strongest electrochemical reads and carry the dilution-correction advantage. Several others start as reagent-pad reads, the same chemistry a dipstick uses, read by the device instead of by eye. Moving more of them to fully quantified electrochemical detection is validation work, not something claimed as finished.
